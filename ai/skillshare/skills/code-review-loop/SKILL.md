# Code Review Loop

Own assessment, repairs, verification, and completion for code, planning, or documentation targets—both standalone and when invoked by `execute-plan` or `ship-plan`. Read `code-review` and the original requirements. Keep the requested target, exclusions, authority, and unrelated work. Do not commit, push, publish comments, change branches, stash, reset, or clean the checkout without authorization.

## Select reviewers

Resolve the reviewer sequence under Reviewer selection (`code-review/references/reviewer-selection.md`, loop mode) and Two-pass review (`code-review/references/two-pass-review.md`). Each sequential stage (internal, Wave 1 external loop/panel, Wave 2 external loop/panel) runs as its own loop invocation with its own round cap (**5 rounds** for code/implementation targets; **3 rounds** for planning/documentation targets); all reviewers within one external panel stage share that stage's round cap.

Examples: `/code-review-loop` (internal), `/code-review-loop claude` (Claude), `/code-review-loop claude, codex, and grok` (one combined panel), `/code-review-loop internal and claude & codex` (internal, then combined panel), `/code-review-loop claude and codex x 2` (Wave 1 Opus + Sol panel, then Wave 2 Astra).

## Coordinate review

Every round reviews under `code-review`. External loops (Codex, Claude, Grok, Gemini) always delegate each reviewer through its one-shot `*-review` skill (`codex-review`, `claude-review`, `grok-review`, `gemini-review`)—never through `*-review-loop`—with the caller's selected provider, pass family, and options. When multiple external reviewers are selected together (any count, such as Claude + Codex + Grok), run them as one combined panel loop rather than separate sequential loops; keep internal review sequential before an external panel when both are requested, and keep two-pass waves sequential under Two-pass review (`code-review/references/two-pass-review.md`). For an internal loop inside T3 Code, use the harness's native subagent controls when the current session's active model and effort already satisfy Reviewer model and effort (`code-review/references/reviewer-settings.md`) and no worktree isolation or explicit setting override is needed; otherwise delegate each round as a fresh `delegate_task` on the current session's provider with explicit model and effort. Without T3 orchestration, delegating internal review is optional—use a separate native reviewer when independent scrutiny adds value or when the main session's settings do not satisfy the reviewer policy. Delegated rounds follow the delegated review runtime (`code-review/references/external-runtime.md`) and Subagent roles and reasoning (`execute-plan/references/subagent-reasoning.md`). Keep ownership clear, and join assigned work before you advance.

Reviewers and repair agents reason from requirements and current evidence; they do not defer to each other's conclusions. Give later rounds only concise prior findings, responses, and open objections, as claims to verify without provider or model attribution; leave out unrelated repair history. Report reviewer independence accurately.

## Assess findings

Assess all completed reviews and any retained partial findings from the round, together with all open findings, before you edit. In a multi-reviewer panel, deduplicate overlapping claims across reviewers first; cross-reviewer agreement is a triage signal, not proof, and every claim is judged on evidence. For each distinct claim, check the requirement or invariant, a reachable trigger, the impact, and attribution to the target. Accept, partially accept, or reject it with a short evidence-based reason. Separate a valid defect from the reviewer's proposed fix. Check disputed facts directly. Do not implement a suggestion only because a reviewer repeats it or gives it high priority, and do not reject a valid defect only because its repair is inconvenient.

Reject unsupported claims, preferences, and scope expansion without routine user confirmation. Resolve material requirement ambiguity from existing authority; ask the user only when still necessary, and continue independent work. Keep a compact ledger in context of findings, decisions, repairs, and check evidence. Partial acceptance keeps the valid part open; a genuinely unresolved claim stays open. Findings from a partial inspection can be assessed, but partial coverage cannot establish a clean result.

## Repair

Group accepted defects across all reviewers in the round by root cause. Before you edit, trace the affected ownership, boundaries, callers, data flow, and invariants, and choose a proportionate correction and its checks. Refactor when local patches would duplicate rules or leave the cause in place. Keep required behavior, data, explicit compatibility, and scope; do not add unrelated redesign or cleanup. Repair agents verify the diagnosis and approach independently, and return evidence for any disagreement instead of implementing a faulty request.

**For planning targets (anti-bloat rule):** resolve valid architectural, requirement, boundary, feasibility, or sequencing findings by tightening the invariant, boundary contract, phase outcome, or observable acceptance criterion—never by pasting prose pseudo-code, wire/byte-level parser specs, internal helper signatures or arities, literal internal tags/keys, exact SQL predicates, or exhaustive test inventories into the plan, unless fixed by an explicit user requirement or external contract (matching `write-plan/references/plan-standards.md`). Reject micro-implementation findings that belong to execution as out-of-layer for planning (adding at most a one-line constraint or acceptance check when a real risk would otherwise be missed).

Finish all repairs, regression coverage, and directly related cleanup for the round and inspect the combined diff before running verification checks. For code targets, verify the original failure and surviving behavior across affected consumers with fast targeted checks during intermediate repair rounds; do not rerun full test suites after every intermediate round. When running as a stage inside `execute-plan`, defer full-suite verification to the closing `final-pass`; in a standalone code loop, run or reuse the required full gates once repairs settle. For saved plans, run any repository document validators and check conformance with `write-plan/references/plan-standards.md` (for session-only scopes, check requirement/boundary/phase/acceptance consistency); for non-plan documentation targets (such as READMEs, guides, runbooks, or skills), verify the document's own contracts, accuracy, references, and repository document validators, without applying saved-plan formatting rules or unrelated application suites. Apply Check evidence and reuse (`final-pass/references/check-evidence.md`): unaffected passing checks can stay valid, and report passing checks to subsequent read-only reviewers so they do not rerun them.

If accepted defects persist after two repair stages, reassess the cumulative changes and shared invariants before the next edit, revise the repair plan, and use a bounded refactor when warranted. If the same finding fails verification (repair checks fail or a reviewer reopens the same claim) twice in the same way, stop repeating local patches: reassess the shared invariant, or when missing authority or input prevents repair, keep it as an open finding or objection with blocker evidence and ask the user or finish the stage as `incomplete` or `capped` under [Finish](#finish) without marking a functioning reviewer `blocked`. Repeated rejected suggestions do not trigger refactoring or scope expansion. This reassessment does not reset the round cap.

## Rounds

Keep review and remediation sequential; do not edit the target during a review. Use at most **5 completed review rounds** per loop stage for code/implementation targets, and at most **3 completed review rounds** per loop stage for planning/documentation targets, regardless of panel size. Failed attempts do not use a round. Parallel inspections launched together against the same unchanged target form one round. The cap is a limit, not a quota.

For each reviewer in the loop, track three orthogonal facts: whether it is `blocked` (unavailable or exhausted attempts), whether it is `open` (not yet reviewed, or has accepted P0–P2 findings, risky/structural repairs, or real unresolved objections not yet verified), and whether it has `broad coverage` (a complete broad review over the target). Normally every reviewer starts non-blocked, `open`, and lacking `broad coverage`. When a caller explicitly invokes a single-pass (or Wave 1) loop to verify localized edits after an equivalent non-blocked broad review of the same target in the same session (such as `ship-plan` Stage 2 after localized post-review plan edits), each matching reviewer that completed that prior broad review starts with `broad coverage` already seeded and `open` set for focused verification of those edits, while any reviewer without that prior broad review starts lacking `broad coverage`; Wave 2 of a two-pass review never uses seeded coverage and always starts broad over the current target including Wave 1 repairs.

- **Round 1:** run every requested non-blocked reviewer in parallel—as a **broad** review of the complete current target with all relevant lenses (`high` effort where warranted under `reviewer-settings.md`) for every reviewer lacking `broad coverage`, or as a **focused** review of the localized edits for reviewers with seeded `broad coverage`. Completing a broad review grants `broad coverage` to that reviewer.
- **Localized repairs vs. structural refactors:** localized repairs (targeted bug fixes, isolated edge cases, doc/test updates) do **not** clear `broad coverage` for already-clean reviewers; only reviewers with repaired open findings or unresolved objections remain `open`, while clean reviewers sit out subsequent rounds. Clear `broad coverage` for all non-blocked reviewers **only** when a repair causes a cross-cutting structural or architectural refactor across the target (such as redesigning a shared boundary, data flow, or multi-module contract).
- **Rounds 2..(cap − 1) (focused by default):** while any non-blocked reviewer is `open`, run **only** the currently `open` non-blocked reviewers in parallel—with a **focused** review of the repairs, open defects, directly affected invariants/callers, and regressions (normally `medium` effort for localized repairs under `reviewer-settings.md`) when that reviewer already holds `broad coverage`, or a **broad** review if it still lacks `broad coverage`. Once no non-blocked reviewer is `open`, if any non-blocked reviewer still lacks `broad coverage` (because it missed Round 1 or a cross-cutting structural refactor cleared `broad coverage`), run one **broad** review across only those reviewers. Do not re-run full-diff broad reviews after routine localized fixes.
- **Final round (Round 3 for plans/docs, Round 5 for code):** no further review rounds can follow the final round—run it focused across remaining `open` non-blocked reviewers that hold `broad coverage`, and broad across any non-blocked reviewer that still lacks `broad coverage`. After the final round completes, safe tail P3 fixes under the P3 convergence rule below are still permitted (and close `clean` if all closing conditions hold, or `incomplete` if a requested reviewer is `blocked`); if any open P0–P2 defect, risky/structural P3, unresolved objection, or missing `broad coverage` among non-blocked reviewers remains after the final round, end `capped` and apply the post-cap recovery rules in [Finish](#finish).
- **P3 convergence (tail-off) rule:**
  - **Planning targets:** every accepted P0–P2 defect (under `code-review`'s plan-review bar) keeps that reviewer `open` until resolved and verified; minor clarity or wording notes belong at P3 (reclassify if needed). When a completed round leaves **0 open P0–P2 defects** across all non-blocked reviewers and only P3 clarity notes remain, fold in safe clarifications directly, verify them with any repository document validators and `plan-standards.md` (or session-scope consistency), and clear `open` for those reviewers without reopening review for them.
  - **Code and non-plan documentation targets:** fix concrete P3s encountered alongside P0–P2 repairs during an active round. Once a completed round leaves **0 open P0–P2 defects** across all non-blocked reviewers and only P3 items remain, apply any clearly safe, localized P3 fixes (such as doc wording, minor UI/tooltip polish, or small test assertions) directly, verify them with checks, and clear `open` without launching another round for those fixes (recording those final P3 fixes as check-verified without fresh review). Reopen focused review for a P3 only when its fix is non-trivial or touches core runtime, security, or data-integrity logic—and only for the reviewer that raised it while rounds remain.
  - **Coverage check before closing:** applying the P3 tail-off rule clears `open`, **not** missing `broad coverage`. After clearing `open`, evaluate the normal closing conditions—if any non-blocked reviewer still lacks `broad coverage` (for example after an earlier structural refactor), run its pending broad review first while rounds remain; if no rounds remain, end `capped` with the missing coverage recorded.

Evidence-based rejection does not need reviewer agreement, or another call only to get a `No findings` verdict. A complete review clears `open` for that reviewer once all of its P0–P2 claims (and any risky/structural P3 claims) are resolved without further review-requiring edits. Keep each reviewer's reported findings and explain your disposition; do not describe a disputed review as a review with no findings. A real unresolved objection keeps that reviewer `open` and prevents a clean close; if an unresolved objection cannot be cleared by authorized edits or existing authority (and no further authorized edits remain), do not re-run reviewers endlessly on an unchanged target—ask the user if needed or stop early and finish the stage as `incomplete` (or `capped` if the objection persists after the final round) with the objection recorded.

### Recover failed attempts

Assess content, not format. Normalize prose, Markdown, or missing bookkeeping yourself without another model call, and keep useful partial findings without inventing missing inspection.

For a model-capacity, rate-limit, API, launch, transport, timeout, authentication, workspace failure, or incomplete required coverage in an otherwise successful response, record the cause and keep useful partial findings. Retry the same round against the unchanged target with the same scope, provider, model, options, and broad or focused purpose. A failed broad review stays a broad review on retry.

Use up to three attempts per round unless the user says otherwise. In a multi-reviewer panel, track attempts and failures per reviewer independently: retry only failed reviewer(s) against the unchanged target without discarding or re-running other reviewers' completed inspections from that round. For transient failures, follow retry-after guidance or use increasing delays within the budget. For configuration or access failures, fix the cause before you retry; mark that reviewer `blocked` if required input or access is unavailable. A user cancellation stops work. Do not duplicate active calls or switch providers silently. If attempts or the time budget run out for a reviewer, mark it `blocked` for the rest of this loop stage and record its cause and any useful partial findings.

Once every reviewer assigned to a round has either completed its inspection or become `blocked`: assess all completed findings together with any retained useful partial findings before deciding whether to repair, schedule another round, or close (never counting a failed or partial inspection toward completed coverage or round count). If at least one reviewer completed an inspection, charge one shared round; if every reviewer assigned to that round became `blocked` (zero completed inspections), do not advance or charge the round—after assessing any retained partial findings, recompute eligibility among any remaining non-blocked reviewers at that same round number (for example, scheduling remaining non-blocked reviewers that still lack broad coverage). If no non-blocked reviewer remains anywhere in the stage (including a single-reviewer loop whose reviewer is blocked), stop the stage as incomplete without advancing past an uncompleted round or claiming that the round cap was reached.

Track round and attempt separately. T3 launch keys and task replacement follow T3 coordination (`code-review/references/t3-runtime.md`); with ACP, use a fresh session after a confirmed failure. A permitted transport fallback stays in the same round and attempt budget.

## Finish

The loop stage is clean when every requested reviewer is non-blocked, has `broad coverage` (retained across localized repairs, or re-established after any structural refactor), and has no `open` P0–P2 findings, risky/structural P3 repairs, or unresolved objections; every finding has an evidence-backed disposition; and repair verification passes (in a standalone code loop, required full checks must pass or reuse valid evidence; inside `execute-plan`, targeted repair checks suffice and the closing `final-pass` owns the final full-suite gates; for saved plans, document validators and `plan-standards.md` checks suffice; for non-plan documentation targets, document validators and contract/reference checks suffice). Safe tail P3 fixes applied under the P3 convergence rule do not prevent a clean close once all non-blocked reviewers hold `broad coverage`—identify them clearly as check-verified without fresh review. When every non-blocked reviewer has cleared `open` and holds `broad coverage` (so no eligible reviewer remains for another round), but one or more requested reviewers are `blocked`, finish the stage as incomplete—preserving each reviewer's status, rounds used, and missing coverage.

After the cap or a real blocker: inside `execute-plan` or `ship-plan`, hand the residual findings and missing coverage to `final-pass` for authorized direct repair and verification; in a standalone loop, complete any authorized direct repairs and verification yourself (or run `final-pass` when appropriate) before concluding. Keep the loop's actual capped or incomplete status, and identify repairs verified without fresh review. A cap ends this review invocation, not the authorized work. Do not call direct inspection a clean review.

During plan execution, follow `execute-plan` (`Completion and review checkpoints`). Report material findings, fixes, rejected claims with reasons, rounds used, per-reviewer resolved model, coverage, and terminal status (for panels), check-verified tail P3 tweaks, checks, and limits concisely. Remove only task-owned scratch and verify the cleanup. Save reports or transcripts only on request.
